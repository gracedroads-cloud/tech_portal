# Emergency Protocol

## Purpose
This emergency protocol defines how the company responds to roadside, fleet, and operational emergencies while preserving the repair-first model.

## Step-by-Step Response
1. Receive incident notification
2. Confirm asset identity, severity, and immediate risk
3. Evaluate whether the issue is serviceable by repair support
4. Identify best technician or repair partner route
5. Trigger dispatch and notify the command center
6. Start GPS tracking and route visibility
7. Monitor the service process in real time
8. Escalate only if safety or operational risk requires additional action
9. Log event details and completion status
10. Confirm resolution and close the service path

## Decision Standard
If a situation can be resolved through repair, technician dispatch, or partner service support, that path is preferred. Tow service is not part of the operating model.

## After-Hours Handling
At night or after standard business hours, the system must route service requests to:
- mobile repair teams
- OEM or dealer repair channels
- heavy-duty maintenance partners
- regional service networks

## Escalation Criteria
Escalate to leadership or senior operations if:
- the asset poses active safety risk
- the repair path is unstable or unavailable
- the customer requires urgent operational decision support
- a critical system outage affects dispatch or GPS visibility

## Required Logging
- incident type
- asset details
- technician assignment
- status updates
- route and ETA information
- resolution summary

## Final Rule
Emergency response is always repair-first, safety-first, and no-tow.
